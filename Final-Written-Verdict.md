For this stock analysis, we developed and determined whether our models can predict Apple's [AAPL] stock better than the naive forecast, which states the price of stock today is the same tomorrow. We started by analyzing several parameters, such as analyzing the stock data against predictors, verifying the quality of the report, understanding the trend of the company stock and what the candlestick reporting has to say about the stock. We also looked to ARIMA and the use of AIC to act as a referee on what parameters should be used to balance simplicity and complexity of the model. We also had the naive test of on Apple's stock to see what it would say againtst seven other predictors. We then use the DM statistic to act as a verifier of what models actually beat the naive test and what was just a stroke of luck, to determine if a model is actually accurate and proper to use. We did also look at Ridge regression and gradient boosting, two models fit with extra information and features to give more context to the prediction and what it outputs. Then finally, we also looked at the horizon and day-ahead forecast to tell how well the model is able to predict into the future at certain periods. 
From this, we have informed statements about how our model performed. 

In the initial testing, the Naive test in dollars under RMSE was 3.2388,, the number to beat and the only model to beat the Naive claim was the Drift model with a score of 3.2372, which is only slightly better. We utilized a walk forward validation which had 29 folds over 1500+ days. moving to ridge regression and gradient boosting, it showed under RMSE, the naive test has a score of 3.2398. ridge reggresion and gradient boosting had higher errors with 3.2431 and 3.2523 respectively. 

Under the four horizon periods, we see that three out of four of them perform worse than the naive test, with only the 63 days performing better by about 0.02. Although this is an improvement, it is not enough basis to say that the output is accurate and the model is qualified for predicting. The final verdict we have to give is that the model is a bit overconfident. We also determined this by forecasting with uncertainty, and determined the model is very uncertain and need to havea a wider range as the day ahead period increases. From this we saw that the share of days when the actual return fell inside the 10% to 90% range was 76.2%, leaving the model to underestimate how often Apple makes sudden big changes that leads to rises or drops in their stock price. However, this model is more suited for testing risks and uncertainty rather than price determination with long-range forecasts.
